About

I work where cosmological theory, statistical inference and scientific computing meet.

Biography

Understanding the high-redshift Universe through its correlations.

I am a PhD researcher in the Department of Physics & Astronomy at University College London and a member of the DESI collaboration. My research centres on the three-dimensional Lyman-α forest, a field of absorption measured along the sightlines to distant quasars.

I am particularly interested in the covariance of Lyman-α correlation functions: how uncertainties vary from bin to bin, why different separations are correlated, and how signal, sparse transverse sampling and spectral noise combine in a realistic survey.

My current work develops an analytical covariance in Fourier space and transforms it into real space using multipole decompositions and 2D FFTLog methods. The broader aim is to make fast, interpretable covariance models available for survey validation, likelihood analyses and Fisher forecasts.

Research interests

  • Lyman-α forest cosmologyLarge-scale structure and cosmic expansion at redshift z ≳ 2.
  • Covariance modellingAnalytical, numerical and mock-based descriptions of correlated uncertainties.
  • Full-shape inferenceExtracting information beyond the baryon acoustic oscillation peak.
  • ForecastingConnecting future survey design to expected cosmological constraints.
